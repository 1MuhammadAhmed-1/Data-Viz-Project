The bottom line

Every fraction of a degree
is measured in human lives.

The data is clear. Climate change is not a future problem. It is a present health crisis — unevenly distributed, deeply tied to inequality, and accelerating. The question is not whether to act. It is how fast.

KEY FINDING 01

PM2.5 exposure remains far above WHO safe limits in several countries, with the heaviest burden concentrated in hotter and lower-access contexts.

KEY FINDING 02

Respiratory and cardiovascular disease rates show strong positive correlations with temperature anomalies — a signal that holds across all income levels.

KEY FINDING 03

Healthcare access is a consistent moderating variable: countries with stronger access tend to show lower respiratory burden under similar climate stress.
